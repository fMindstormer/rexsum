How a regex does the arithmetic

A check-digit rule reads the digits and keeps a running sum modulo 10 or 11. That is a finite automaton, so an equivalent regular expression must exist. The textbook conversion makes it huge, and common advice says a regex cannot do this at all. Three steps bring it down to a size a form can carry.

37,396,808characters for Luhn without the split below

1. The direct route explodes

Converting an automaton to a regex removes its states one by one, and every removal can multiply the expression. Luhn's automaton has 20 states; even a one-line rule like "divisible by 11" comes out at 3,928,634 characters.

10 = 2 × 5so check mod 2 and mod 5 separately (Chinese remainder theorem)

2. Split the modulus

A sum is 0 mod 10 exactly when it is 0 mod 2 and 0 mod 5. A JavaScript lookahead intersects two regexes, (?=A$)B, so each part gets its own small automaton. For Luhn, the mod-2 part needs 198 characters; nearly all the size is in the mod-5 part.

24,246characters for Luhn's mod-5 part

3. Eliminate in the right order

The order in which states are removed decides the size. Removing the in-between positions first lets 5 states read two digits at a time, and the best of their 120 orders is kept. For short fixed lengths, splitting the number at the best position, recursively, does better: EAN-8 needs 3,971 characters.

The mod-2 part of Luhn, in full (the rightmost digit counts once, the next one doubled):

(?:[02468]|[13579](?:[0-4][02468]|[5-9][13579])*(?:[0-4][13579]|[5-9][02468]))?(?:(?:[0-4][13579]|[5-9][02468])(?:[0-4][02468]|[5-9][13579])*(?:[0-4][13579]|[5-9][02468])|[0-4][02468]|[5-9][13579])*

IBAN: too big for a pure regex

IBANs are checked mod 97. That is prime, so nothing splits, and the constructions above grow like n7.6 in the number of digits, and a German IBAN would need over 108 characters. No pure regex does much better: counting shows that every one needs at least 55 million. But a JavaScript regex has more syntax than a textbook regular expression. A group captured inside a lookahead keeps its value, and a backreference can test it later, so a group can serve as a register: capturing the first v characters stores the number v. Uezato used the same device in 2024 to prove exactly what such regexes can recognize (see below).

(?=(...))          stores 3: the group captures the first 3 characters
(?=\5(?<=^...))    tests whether group 5 holds 3

Each IBAN pattern is a row of such lookaheads. Each step reads one character and writes the new remainder into fresh groups, the way you would compute it on paper. The remainder is held as two small numbers, a ≤ 13 and b ≤ 9, standing for 10a + b and never reduced: since 100 leaves 3 mod 97, a digit d turns (a, b) into (b + ⌊(3a + d) / 10⌋, (3a + d) mod 10). A letter counts as two digits. The German pattern has 59,966 characters and 69 groups, and the size grows linearly with the length.

Backreferences make the syntax more expressive than regular expressions, but these patterns do not need that power: each covers one country, so its strings have one fixed length, and the language is finite and therefore regular. What the backreferences buy is size. A computed remainder is stored once and reused, where a pure regex has to spell out every path to it: 59,966 characters for Germany instead of at least 55 million.

Every pattern

Each pattern was tested against a reference implementation on 23,000 to 27,000 strings in Python, and on about 790 more, edge cases included, in the regex engines of Chromium (V8) and Safari (JavaScriptCore). The same strings were entered into a real <input> with JavaScript off in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, and in Safari's own engine. No mismatches. Times are for V8 in Node with the v flag the pattern attribute uses. In a browser, the first keystroke compiles the pattern and later ones take microseconds; Safari compiles it again at every keystroke, in under 5 ms. Compressed, the patterns shrink 8 to 45 times: GitHub Pages sends this page, 360 kB with eleven fields, as 30 kB.

Limits

A prime modulus cannot be split, and the size grows fast with it. Digits whose sum is a multiple of 5 already need stars nested five deep (McNaughton's theorem on star height), and the constructions here grow like 4q−1 for modulus q.

Mod 97 as a pure regex

Eight-digit multiples of 97 already need 100,133 characters, and any pure regex for a German IBAN needs at least 55 million (the README has the proof). The IBAN patterns here use capture groups instead, and accept only the electronic form: capitals, no spaces.

Damm check digit

Its table is a quasigroup, not a group, so nothing splits. The direct conversion gives 1,398,101 characters. Capture registers should handle it as they handle IBAN; not tried yet.

ISBN-10 is the largest pure regex

Mod 11 is prime, so its pattern is one piece of 91,659 characters. The first keystroke compiles it: about 0.2 seconds in Chromium.

What existed before

  • Validation guides say a regex can check a card number's format but not its Luhn digit, and tools call code after the match.
  • Steven Pemberton wrote the Luhn check as an Invisible XML grammar (CWI, 2026): 20 states read right to left. It is a grammar, not a regex.
  • Can a regex match valid card numbers? (2025) converts a Luhn automaton directly: 32,461,605 (even lengths) and 48,236,673 (odd) characters, which grep and ripgrep cannot load. rexsum's pattern is 1,500 times smaller.
  • Regexes for "divisible by 7" are known puzzles; generators rely on a plain conversion in JFLAP or on recursive PCRE, which browsers do not support.
  • Gruber and Holzer survey the theory: From Finite Automata to Regular Expressions and Back, including elimination-order heuristics and lower bounds.
  • Yuya Uezato, Regular Expressions with Backreferences and Lookaheads Capture NLOG (ICALP 2024), stores numbers as the lengths of captures and tests them with backreferences inside lookaheads, the device behind the IBAN patterns, to prove that such regexes recognize exactly the problems solvable in nondeterministic logarithmic space. Its constructions loop over the captures; JavaScript resets a repeated group on every iteration, so here the steps are written out.

rexsum compiles any weighted check-digit sum: python -m rexsum custom --weights 1,3 --mod 10 --length 13
